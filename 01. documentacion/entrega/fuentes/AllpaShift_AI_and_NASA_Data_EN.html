Use of AI and NASA Data

Which NASA data we use, how we use it, and how we use artificial intelligence.

NASA data

AllpaShift uses six open NASA datasets. Every calculation (indicators, season risk, rotation scores and alerts) comes from these data and the crop and soil catalog through fixed rules, with no AI.

DatasetVariablesHow it contributes
NASA POWER (API, Agroclimatology)PRECTOTCORR, GWETTOP, T2M_MAX, T2M_MIN; daily data and climatologyHeat indicator and rainfall/soil moisture at any coordinate. Its climatology defines "normal" and the risk of each month of the season.
GPM IMERG V07: Final monthly (GPM_3IMERGM), Final daily (GPM_3IMERGDF), Late daily (GPM_3IMERGDL)precipitationCurrent satellite rainfall in the Piura pilot, monthly series 2001–2026 for validation, and day-by-day rain of any month.
SMAP L3 Enhanced (SPL3SMP_E V006, 9 km)soil_moisture (AM and PM passes)Current soil moisture versus its 2016–2025 normal. Drives the "wait / irrigate" action, water penalties and the local soil picture.
MODIS Terra NDVI (MOD13Q1 V061, 250 m)250m_16_days_NDVIVegetation indicator: crop vigor versus the normal for the same 16-day period; low vigor or bare soil signals stress and soil-cover needs.
MUR SST v4.1 (NASA JPL, PO.DAAC, via NOAA ERDDAP)analysed_sst, sstAnom over Niño 1+2El Niño indicator: raises rain risk from January to April, triggers "prepare drainage" and dates the 2017 alert.

Partner space agencies: we do not use partner agency data. NOAA ERDDAP is only the access channel for MUR SST, a NASA JPL product. Local soil information comes from the farmer (soil type and salinity), with a soil type suggested by SoilGrids 2.0 (ISRIC, CC-BY 4.0), always labelled as not NASA data. Crop traits (water use, temperature range, family, root depth, salt tolerance) come from the team's catalog, built from FAO-56, FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 29 and Peruvian agricultural sources listed in References.

How we use the data, step by step

  1. The farmer picks a plot and priorities; the app sends them to the AllpaShift API.
  2. If a fresh result is stored, the API answers from the database (the demo does not depend on NASA servers being up).
  3. Otherwise the calculation service reads the NASA datasets for that point and compares each value with its normal: low, medium or high.
  4. The climatology, local soil and crop traits are used to estimate season risk and to score rotations for climate and soil health, ranked by the farmer's priorities.
  5. The API stores the result and every NASA value used; the app shows each result with the dataset it comes from.

If a value is not available for a place or date, the app shows "no data" and never replaces it with invented values.

Use of artificial intelligence

We use AI at two points: inside the app, to explain recommendations, and during development, to support the team. AI never calculates the indicators, scores or alerts.

ToolPurposeHow we applied it
Groq with gpt-oss-20b (Apache 2.0)In-app assistant that explains the recommendation in plain words.It receives the already-calculated recommendation as JSON plus the farmer's question. It is told not to calculate, not to invent numbers and to cite only the sources in the JSON; numbers in the answer are checked. If it fails, the app shows the calculated reason. The API key stays on the server.
Claude (Anthropic)Writing part of the code (frontend, API, calculation service, mobile) and documentation drafts.The team defined the architecture, rules and data. It reviewed, tested and fixed the generated code, and verified it with automated tests.
ChatGPT (OpenAI)Visual support: cover image and AllpaShift icon.Visual illustration only; it does not represent real data.
