High-Level Summary

Plant for the climate that's coming, with NASA satellite data.

What is the project?

AllpaShift is a web and mobile app (Android and iOS) that turns NASA Earth observations into a simple decision for any farmer: what to plant, when to plant it, and what to change when extreme weather is coming. The farmer picks a plot on a risk map, says what matters most to them, and the app answers in plain language:

  • What to do now. One action for this fortnight (plant, wait, irrigate or protect), based on current field conditions.
  • Season risk. Month-by-month risk from planting to harvest against drought, extreme rain, heat and El Niño.
  • What to change. Crop rotations scored from 0 to 100 by climate risk and by their effect on soil health (legumes, crop families, root depth, soil cover, no monoculture, salinity), using the soil type and salinity of the plot, and weighted by the farmer's own priority: take less risk, save water or care for the soil. For permanent crops such as mango or lemon, it suggests actions for each growth stage.
  • Why. Every result shows the NASA dataset behind it, and an AI assistant explains it in simple words without doing any of the calculations.

It works at any coordinate on Earth. Piura, Peru, is our pilot.

Why does it matter?

Millions of small farmers still decide what to plant out of habit. When the climate changes, they lose more than a harvest: they lose a whole year of investment and, with repeated monoculture, the health of their soil. In 2017 the coastal El Niño flooded the fields of Piura, and farmers had no way to see it coming. The data needed to anticipate these events already exists and is free, but it does not reach the field.

How does it address the challenge?

Field Shift asks for a decision-support tool that combines NASA Earth observations with local soil information, crop characteristics and farmer priorities to explore rotations that strengthen soil health. AllpaShift brings those four pieces together. Six NASA datasets drive it: NASA POWER (temperature, rainfall, climatology), GPM IMERG (satellite rainfall, monthly and daily), SMAP (soil moisture), MODIS NDVI (crop vigor) and MUR SST (sea temperature, the early El Niño signal). Applying today's rules to real 2017 data, AllpaShift would have raised the El Niño alert weeks before the peak rains, and it shows the rain of every month, day by day, since 2001.

We don't predict the weather. We prepare farmers, and their soil, for it.
